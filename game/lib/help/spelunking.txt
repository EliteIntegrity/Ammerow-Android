Side-View Caves
===============

These caves are turn-based locations with their own movement rules.  A wrong
direction can step away from support and begin a real fall.  Read the stamina,
posture and Air display; animation never changes the resolved outcome.

The status strip along the bottom keeps HP, stamina, air and movement
separate from messages, with the cave section beneath them and, when
climbing, the stamina each direction costs.  It stays in view while
fishing.

Movement and safety
-------------------

D-pad               move, climb, descend, swim, or choose a jump direction
Centre of d-pad     wait one turn

The large button follows your situation:

  Jump    standing: begin the multi-stage jump (the d-pad's down after a
          jump abandons the reach and drops straight down)
  Grip    climbing, hanging, falling or at the top of a jump: cycle or set
          an adjacent grip target (free)
  Wait    swimming
  Get     pick up the objects here
  Up      take the passage up here; Down takes a passage down
  Leave   leave the cave by the way out under you
  Disarm  beside a known armed trap, or a chest known to be trapped
  Open    beside a known closed door, or beside or on a supply chest or
          an unopened discovery
  Fish    beside water the cave can be fished from

Around it are Grip (Piton when Grip is the large button; Jump when
Disarm, Open or Fish is), Rope and Peek.  Piton hammers a piton at your
position; Rope deploys rope downward from the current piton.  Taps on the
cave view do nothing: caves are moved through with the d-pad.  More has
every cave action under Caves, Rest and Tunnel among them.

Bare-rock gripping costs much more stamina than rope.  Stable ground allows
recovery by waiting; hanging from a piton does not.  A piton can support the
start of a jump, and a valid grip can arrest the rise or the held
ledge-reaching pause.  Diagonal grips and deliberate release are allowed.
Falls beyond a short safe distance deal sharply increasing damage and may
kill immediately.

From rope or a piton you can step diagonally up onto an adjoining
supported ledge without choosing a grip first.  A diagonal step down can
land on a piton or catch a rope; without support, you fall.  Hanging from
a piton still does not restore stamina.

Peek
----

Peek is not Look.  Keep one finger on Peek and press a direction on the
d-pad with another: you look one tile from a safe offset viewpoint without
moving.  Without Peek held, the same direction is a movement attempt.
Lifting your finger returns the viewpoint while keeping legitimately seen
cells.  Ordinary sight shows only the first part of a drop; a downward
diagonal Peek can reveal a long clear shaft and its enclosing rock faces.
Visible passages keep their continuous floors and ceilings, but rock still
blocks creatures, objects and open space around corners.  Menus and Back
clear Peek.

Survey doors
------------

Occasional dry passages contain old survey doors.  Stand beside one on
stable ground: Open (the large button there, or under More) opens it and
Close door (More) closes it.  A sole suitable door beside you is chosen
automatically; otherwise choose its direction with the d-pad.  Each
attempt is manual.  Locked store doors use your ordinary physical
disarming skill.  Opening, closing or failing to pick a lock spends a
normal turn; attempts that cannot work spend none.  There is no
lock-picking experience, and a closed, unlocked door stays unlocked.

Closed doors block movement, sight and shots.  Cave creatures cannot open
them.  A creature or loose item in the doorway prevents closing it.  Look
tells open, closed and discovered locked doors apart; remembered doors
show their last known state.  Mapping reveals doors but not their locks
or hidden occupants.

Traps and survey chests
-----------------------

New cave sections can contain a sparse spring-spike or stone-release
trap and an old supply chest on dry ground, away from the guaranteed
route.  Searching happens as you move or wait: enough searching skill
reveals a nearby trap.  Looking or peeking gives no free searching
attempts.  Trap-detection magic also reveals traps; object detection finds
chests but does not tell you whether they are trapped.  Mapping alone
reveals neither.

Disarm (the large button there, or under More) works on a known trap
beside you while you stand on stable ground, or on its square.  With more
than one candidate, choose with the d-pad: left, right, or its centre for
your own square.  Each attempt is manual and costs one normal turn;
failure can spring the trap.  Successful disarming awards 1 experience,
once only.  Blindness, confusion and hallucination make disarming harder.
These physical mechanisms strike once; walking, landing or teleporting
onto an armed floor trap fires it.  Spent traps are harmless and marked
with a dim ^.

Open works beside or on a chest.  Picking its lock uses ordinary
disarming skill.  Opening a trapped chest springs the mechanism first;
open it again to retrieve the supplies if you survive.  Disarming a chest
trap does not pick its lock.  The supplies are placed on the ground for
Get.  An empty chest is marked _; it cannot be closed, reset or looted
again.  Existing saves keep their explored sections unchanged; new
fixtures appear only in newly generated sections.

Small discoveries off the route
-------------------------------

Some newly explored sections contain an abandoned survey camp, a ledge
cache or a specimen niche on a dry shelf.  They are optional detours, not
mandatory stops.  Look describes the find.  Stand beside it or on it and
press Open (the large button there): the contents go onto the ground for
Get.  Reading its note again is free (Open under More, Caves), but
revisiting or reloading never replenishes its supplies.

These named discoveries are unlocked and untrapped.  That does not make
every chest safe: ordinary survey supply chests may still have locks and
traps.  Camps hold modest supplies; ledge caches can hold healing;
specimen niches hold survey curios.  A Brass Survey Counter, Clouded
Survey Lens or Knotted Stone Sample can be sold at the Fishing Shop.  They
have no secret powers and are not needed for a quest.  The short notes
tell you about earlier expeditions.

Rats, skitters or beetles sometimes occupy the shelf beyond a find.  They
are present before you open it, not an ambush conjured by taking the
supplies.  Look and Peek before committing; keep a clear retreat.  You can
leave the find alone.  Not every section has a discovery, and visited
sections are not restocked.

Tunnelling and terrain
----------------------

Stand on a stable rock floor, choose Tunnel under More, then a direction
on the d-pad.  Each attempt takes one turn and the ordinary digging
stamina cost, shown in Look and in the digging message.  Attempts do not
repeat by themselves.  Your best carried digger is used for the attempt
unless your weapon cannot be removed.  Strength, digging skill and
equipment matter: shale is easier than limestone; iron-stained stone is
hardest.  An unsuitable tool, exhaustion or an unsafe cut spends no turn
or stamina.

A finished tunnel removes one rock cell, not the whole stratum.  It
immediately updates sight and removes formations that lost their
support.  Cutting out your floor can cause a damaging or fatal fall;
loose items and ground creatures also fall when their supporting rock is
removed.  An anchored rope can catch you if you still have stamina.  You
cannot tunnel while hanging.

Outer bedrock, pool-retaining rock, fixture foundations, a piton's last
brace and rock securing the established return route cannot be
excavated.  Cuts that would leave loose loot underwater or occupants
colliding are also refused.  Water does not flow and whole caverns do not
collapse.  Very old caves without a certified return route stay
protected against excavation.

Stone to Mud on its own and short wall-removal rays follow the same
safety rules and remove the first exposed rock in range.  Magic keeps its
normal costs; a protected rock resists it.  Earthquakes, destruction and
chained wall-removal effects remain unavailable here.  Excavated terrain
and fallen items persist when you leave the section or save and return;
visited caves are never generated again.

Rest and nearby lists
---------------------

Rest (More) rests as needed: until HP, focus, stamina and held air are
full and harmful conditions have passed.  Rest does not refill scuba.
The ordinary world clock, hunger, healing, status decay and hazards
continue.  Stamina recovers at the same rate as deliberate one-turn
waiting, not twice.

Rest needs dry, stable ground.  It stops for sighted or detected
creatures, hunger, damage, unsafe footing, or any button you press.  It
will not start on a known armed trap or while magical travel is pending;
wait with the d-pad's centre instead.  Unseen, undetected traps and
creatures are not revealed by its safety check.

Monsters and Items seen (More) list only the creatures and items in sight
or detected in this section, with how far above, below, left or right of
you each is: not a safe walking route.  Items in the same pile are listed
separately, unknown flavours stay unknown and ignored items are left out.
The d-pad scrolls, PgUp and PgDn turn pages and Back returns; no time
passes.  The lists neither travel nor pick up.  While hallucinating you
cannot tell nearby occupants apart.

Pickup and ignoring
-------------------

Stepping onto loose objects uses your ordinary automatic-pickup settings.
"Always pickup items" takes precedence; otherwise !g prevents pickup, =g
asks for it, and =gN limits the number carried.  "Always pickup items
matching inventory" also follows the first matching pack stack's
inscriptions.  A full pack only takes what fits in existing stacks; the
rest stays here.  Nothing is taken from neighbouring squares, and there
is no automatic cave travel or fighting.

Get picks up what is here.  Handling a batch of cave items takes one
normal action; collecting gold alone automatically takes no extra time.
Hazards and creatures continue meanwhile.  Waiting or dropping does not
pick anything up.  Chest contents stay on their square until you pick
them up or step onto them.

Ignore (in an item's actions, or More) ignores an item or an offered
known category.  Ignored items disappear from the cave view, Look and the
nearby lists; they are not destroyed.  Show ignored (More) shows them
again so you can unignore or recover them.  Ignored gear you carry is
dropped here when safe.  !d and !* protect against that; equipped items
need confirmation and stuck items cannot be removed.  If the square
cannot hold dropped objects, or cave storage is full, you keep them.

Cave creatures
--------------

Dry shelves are home to chasm skitters, quick but fragile cave rats and
slower, hard-shelled stone beetles.  These ground creatures cannot follow
you across an open shaft or through a pool.  Look at a visible creature
for its health and description.  Bump attacks need stable footing; fire,
throw or use an adapted spell when appropriate.  Cleared cave sections do
not replenish their creatures.

Cave creatures have explicit traits.  All current kinds are animals;
stone beetles resist poison and cannot be confused.  Effective weapon
brands and slays work in melee and ranged attacks.  Sleep, hold, slow,
haste, confusion, stun and fear affect cave creatures; Look and the
creature list show their current conditions.  Damage wakes a sleeping
creature.

Creature teleportation needs an empty dry shelf.  Polymorph changes
creatures within the cave's own kinds, not into an arbitrary dungeon
monster.  Supported summons are hostile, need nearby space, and give no
kill experience.  Polymorph cannot raise a creature's original
experience award.  These conditions survive saving.  Probing and
sight-wide magic need sight; detection alone does not let a spell hit
through rock.  Banishment gives no experience and still strains your
health.

Cave Survey
-----------

Char, then the Cave Survey tab.  OK, or a tap on a section, explores the
survey: the d-pad then selects sections in the diagram, and Back returns
to the tabs (Back again closes the screen).  The @ marks where you
actually are, not the highlighted section.  Inspecting a section never
moves you or spends a turn.  The diagram shows connections and survey
depths, not compass directions or exact entrance positions.  Only
discovered sections and passages appear.  Section numbers refer to the
current chart, which may rescale and renumber as you discover more.

Shared equipment and survival
-----------------------------

Inv, Char and the More drawer work here as they do above ground: wield,
take off, inscribe, refuel, drop, eat, quaff, read, use devices, activate,
browse, study, cast, fire and throw.  Look uses the cave-native Look
cursor.

Spellbooks must be carried.  Studying remains possible for every normal spell,
including one whose effect only works outside a side-view cave.  Casting keeps
the normal focus cost, failure chance and turn cost.  Aimed spells use the
eight directions of the d-pad; they collide with cave rock and creatures.
Effects without side-view meaning are refused before focus or a turn is
spent.

Scuba gear supplies a visible reserve underwater.  When it is exhausted,
ordinary Breath drains until you surface.  Surface recovery does not recharge
the gear.

Commands which still need side-view spatial rules are refused rather than
silently using a top-down target.  These include conventional targeting,
the ordinary dungeon-map view and automatic cave navigation.  Cave Survey
and cave-native Look are the deliberate side-view alternatives to the two
map screens.
